01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

Small scope.
Deep systems.

Orynqeth Survivors is a top-down, auto-attacking survival game with ten-minute runs, escalating enemy pressure, weapon upgrades, passive tomes, and persistent unlocks.

I used the project to practice end-to-end technical game design: defining rules and balance, shaping data-driven systems, designing editor workflows, validating edge cases, and directing AI-assisted implementation.

Contribution transparency: I led game/system design, specifications, balancing decisions, visual direction, integration review, and validation. A substantial part of the C# implementation and asset generation was produced with AI tools and iterated under my direction.

03 / GAME SYSTEMS

Designing for decisions.

The core systems are built to create different short-run strategies while remaining configurable.

COMBAT

Four weapon identities

Magic Bolt is the reliable starting projectile. Orbiting Blades reward positioning, Chain Lightning handles groups, and Meteor delivers delayed area damage.

Distinct targeting, timing and spatial roles
PROGRESSION

Controlled random choices

Each level-up presents three upgrades with at most one weapon option. Rarity weights and Luck influence the quality of offers; tomes expand long-term build direction.

Rarity · Luck · Weapons · Passive tomes
PACING

Escalation and horde waves

Enemy frequency increases throughout a run. Periodic horde events inject sharp pressure spikes into the pacing curve rather than relying on continuous growth alone.

Time-driven density · Event windows
META

Progress beyond one run

Weapons and tomes enter the Collection through unlock conditions. Persistent statistics track completed runs, combat performance and progression across sessions.

Collection · Unlocks · Run history

05 / TECHNICAL DESIGN

Built to be iterated.

Data, lifecycle, and repeatability mattered as much as moment-to-moment combat.

01

Deterministic procedural terrain

Forest tiles and decorations are generated around the camera in reusable chunks. Deterministic placement includes negative world coordinates and chunk boundaries.

9pooled chunks observed
02

Sprite and effect lifecycles

Animation state resets on reuse; projectiles orient to velocity; lightning and meteor visuals follow their damage timing without changing combat rules.

26environment PNG assets
03

Targeted verification

Automated runtime/editor checks cover UI, save isolation, pooling and progression. In a reported environment benchmark, 1,000 stationary updates produced no measured managed allocations.

These numbers describe specific validation runs, not a blanket guarantee of zero allocations or a full production performance certification. A complete GPU/FPS stress profile remains outside the documented checks.

06 / CHALLENGES & OUTCOME

Iteration is the work.

01 / BALANCE

One weapon dominated.

Meteor's area damage overshadowed other builds. I reviewed damage-by-weapon results and adjusted the starting bolt and competing weapon values to give each path a stronger role.

02 / GAME FEEL

The pacing felt flat.

Linear enemy scaling lacked tension. Increasing spawn frequency and inserting horde waves produced more visible peaks in enemy pressure.

03 / PRODUCTION

Visual regressions slowed progress.

UI overlaps, pooled animations, screenshot gamma issues, and sprite integration problems led to repeatable editor checks and scenario-specific captures.

THE RESULT

A playable prototype and a repeatable way to demonstrate it.

The Windows game launches and the portfolio capture presets generate curated scenes. The project documents not only systems shipped but the tools, decisions, and verification practices that made rapid iteration possible.
